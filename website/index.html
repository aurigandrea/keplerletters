Digital Humanities meets the History of Astronomy

Visualising the Kepler Correspondence

Using Digital Humanities approaches in the history of astronomy, this project explores explores both the social network and the geographical footprint of Johannes Kepler's correspondence. The dataset is based on records from Early Modern Letters Online (EMLO).

Start Exploring

Understanding the Visualisations

What are these visualisations?

You can browse three views of Kepler's correspondence network (1590–1631). Each visualisation reveals different aspects of his social connections: with whom he connected the most, where he received and sent letters his letters, and when his correspondence was the most productive.

Rapid Dataset Overview

  • 1,154 letters from EMLO
  • 242 unique individuals
  • 330 correspondence pairs
  • 137 locations
  • Time span: 1590–1631
